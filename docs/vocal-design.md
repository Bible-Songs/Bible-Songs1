Source: https://digital.abcaudio.com/news/aretha-franklin-tops-rolling-stones-200-greatest-singers-all-time-list

This list is a reference for the user's request, not a training dataset or a claim that the new voice reproduces those singers. The ranked article itself was unavailable for full inspection. No performer recordings were analyzed, downloaded or cloned, and the model was not trained or fine-tuned during this task.

## Original performance brief

The broadly applicable goals are clear consonants, a warm central register, coherent phrases, restrained vibrato, controlled word emphasis, and more contrast between verse and refrain. These are general vocal techniques, not traits exclusive to any performer. Berklee's voice-technique material describes phrasing guided by the text, dynamics, straight tones and vibrato: https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/singing-lessons-to-develop-your-sound-and-style/. Its jazz singing curriculum covers tone, articulation, dynamics and interpretation: https://online.berklee.edu/courses/jazz-singing-101.

The implementation uses a generic openly released Kokoro voice and original local audio processing. It does not claim to have learned the identities of ten singers, to sound like any one of them, or to produce professional-quality singing. The app uses the name “Warm expressive” and clearly labels it experimental.

## App-facing creation notes

**Classic synthetic:** Free eSpeak formant voice, with words pitched and timed to the original melody, then mixed with our backing track. Deliberately robotic. Original audio file unchanged.

**Warm expressive:** Free local Kokoro standard voice, with vowel sustains, preserved consonant edges, phrase dynamics and light vibrato. Speech-to-singing experiment; not a human recording, artist clone or celebrity blend.

Both methods use original adapted lyrics and an original melody; Scripture reference remains the public-domain Berean Standard Bible.
